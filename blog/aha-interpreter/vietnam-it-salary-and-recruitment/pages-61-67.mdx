- From the survey, Full-time IT freelancing is

highly motivated by the desire for flexible

working hours (69.2%) and the ability to work

remotely (50.2%), promising increased income

potential. However, pursuing this path requires a

shift beyond technical expertise. Success

demands strong competencies in ownership,

client management, and financial skills to

address the top challenges: ensuring a stable

income (34.0%) and mitigating risks such as late

payment or non-payment by clients (22.0%).

- The most secure path forward for IT freelancers

is a dual-responsibility model - maintaining a

full-time role while taking on side projects. This

approach is already embraced by 45.3% of

surveyed IT freelancers, who plan to continue

freelancing alongside their full-time jobs. This

strategy not only accelerates skill growth but

also strengthens the most crucial source for

freelancing IT projects: 46.2% from

acquaintances, friends, and relatives. The data

reinforces a clear message that relationship

building is essential for long-term career

readiness in the freelance IT market.

**IT Student Readiness for the Future Workforce**

**Strong** **academic** **focus** **is** **evident,** **but** **their** **readiness** **in** **practical** **skills** **remains** **limited,**
**resulting** **in** **skill** **gaps** **and** **increasing** **job** **search** **anxiety** **among** **IT** **students.**

**Strong** **academic** **focus** **but** **limited** **practical**

**preparation.**

IT students invest heavily in academics, with 54.1 %

aiming for the highest GPA and 50.3 % taking extra

courses. However, real world preparation is low, as only

28.6 % gain freelance experience, 26.4 % join projects or

hackathons, and 18.3 % participate in soft skill clubs. This

reveals a clear gap between academic effort and

practical readiness.

**English** **improvement** **is** **a** **priority,** **but** **skills** **remain**

**basic.**

Improving English is the most common preparation

activity, reported by 59.1% of IT students, yet self
evaluated proficiency remains at a basic level on

average (1.68/5). This shows students recognize English

as a major barrier when entering the job market,

especially for technical communication and teamwork.

**Job-readiness** **anxiety** **driven** **by** **skill** **gaps** **and**

**market** **pressure** **(see** **data** **in** **next** **page).**

Students express strong concerns about their readiness

due to technical gaps, weak soft skills, and rising hiring

expectations. Many feel their practical ability and

confidence are not enough to meet employer demands,

even for entry level roles.

**IT Student Readiness for the Future Workforce**

**English** **Competency:** **A** **significant** **gap** **remains** **between** **university-level** **English** **training** **and**
**the** **practical** **communication** **skills** **required** **for** **IT** **careers.**

**Passive** **learning** **dominates** **even** **though** **most** **students** **are**

**in their final year.**

Most students in the survey are in their final year, yet their

English learning habits are still dominated by passive methods

such as reading or watching movies. These approaches improve

exposure but do not build the active communication skills

required for real IT work. Students should be having more

structured and practical trainings for IT-related English usage.

**Key** **English** **skills** **are** **considered** **averagely** **basic** **despite**

**students being close to graduation.**

Final year students still rate themselves mostly at Basic level in

writing, speaking, listening, and presentation skills. Writing and

public speaking are the weakest areas.

**The** **gap** **between** **university** **English** **requirements**

**and** **job** **market** **expectations** **becomes** **urgent** **for**

**graduating students.**

Even with English requirements in place at universities, students

still rate their real-world English capabilities as low. The gap is

most visible in practical skills needed for IT work such as writing

reports, presenting ideas, and communicating in teams. This

reinforces the need for both institutions and students to invest

more intentionally in English competency for global careers.
